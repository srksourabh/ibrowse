Accessibility · Chrome extension

iBrowse

Hear any website. Explore it by mouse, touch, keyboard and voice, built for blind and low-vision people.

Free and open source · github.com/srksourabh/ibrowse

Why it matters

The web is built to be looked at.

Layout tells sighted people where things are: the menu on the left, the offers on the right, the button under the picture. Without sight, that map is missing.

2.2 billionpeople live with a near or distance vision impairment (WHO, World report on vision, 2019).
Where?Screen readers read in order. iBrowse adds where: what is under your hand, and what is on each side.
Any siteNo changes to websites needed. iBrowse reads the page you are on.

How it works · in five steps

Turn it on, listen, explore, talk, act.

Nothing happens until you turn Explore Mode on. Off, the page behaves exactly as usual.

Page briefing

A map of the page, spoken.

iBrowse finds the header, the side panels, the main content and the footer, from landmarks or from the layout itself, and says what each one holds.

iBrowse saysHeader: search box, 4 links. Left panel, Categories: 3 links. Main content, Cherry tomatoes: 2 paragraphs, 1 picture, 2 buttons. Right panel, Offers: 2 links. Want me to read it?

Explore by pointer and touch

Whatever is under your hand, named.

🖱

Hover

"Add to basket — Button, Clickable." Speech stops the moment you move on.

🎧

Sound in space

Short earcons mark links, buttons, text and edges, panned left or right to match where they are.

☝

Touch

Drag a finger to explore, double-tap to activate, and hear an edge tone at the side of the screen.

💬

Rest to ask

Rest on something and iBrowse asks "What would you like to do?". Answer out loud.

Reading

Click to read. Scroll word by word.

Click a paragraph to hear it. Over text, the mouse wheel steps one word at a time, Shift + wheel spells a word, and Alt+Shift+R reads on from where you are.

Voice conversation

Can't click? Say it.

"Click""Click add to basket""Scroll down""Scroll up" "What is on the left?""Read the page""Next page""Go back" "Find the search box""Where am I?""Stop""Help"

Commands are understood on your computer. Anything else is a question, answered from the page if you turn on AI.

You sayClick buy now.

iBrowse saysBuy now. Say yes to press it, or no.

You sayYes.

Anything that can't be taken back (paying, sending, deleting, agreeing) waits for a spoken yes, and a harmless label alone can't hide it.

Plays well with others

Your screen reader, your hardware.

🗣

Companion mode

NVDA speaks for iBrowse, so you hear one voice. Checked with NVDA: the briefing and hover descriptions were spoken once, and push to talk worked while NVDA ran.

🎮

Feel it

Every sound has a matching touch effect: a rumbling game controller, or a USB haptic device described in JSON.

🧩

Open SDK

Hardware makers get a schema, an example profile, an example driver and conformance checks.

Private by default

It stays on your computer.

No servers, no accounts, no analytics. Optional Google Gemini features (a natural voice, AI summaries and answers, image descriptions) use your own key. iBrowse says what will be sent before you allow it.

Never sent: form values, passwords, cookies, browsing history, or anything while Explore Mode is off.

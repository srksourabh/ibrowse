# Acceptable use policy

iBrowse is free software under the [MIT License](LICENSE). This policy describes how we ask the project and its name to be used. It does not restrict the licence.

## Intended use

iBrowse is built to help blind and low-vision people read and operate the web, and to let hardware makers connect haptic devices. Please use it for that.

## Please do

- Use it on sites you are allowed to visit.
- Keep your Gemini key private, and watch your own usage and billing with Google.
- Tell users what a modified build does with their data.
- Test with real users and, where it matters, with a screen reader.

## Please do not

- Use it to scrape sites in breach of their terms, to bypass paywalls or access controls, or to automate abuse.
- Use the AI features to process data you have no right to share with Google.
- Ship a fork that collects data without a clear opt-in, and still call it iBrowse.
- Present iBrowse as a medical, mobility or safety device. It is a convenience tool. Do not rely on it where a mistake could hurt someone, such as banking confirmations, medical dosing or emergencies, without a second check.

## Honest limits

- Descriptions of clickability, images and summaries can be wrong. iBrowse says "Maybe" when it is guessing.
- It works on pages Chrome lets extensions run on. It cannot read `chrome://` pages, the Web Store, or PDFs in Chrome's viewer.
- Local voices cannot be panned; stereo placement uses sounds and cloud voices.

## Name and logo

"iBrowse" may be used to refer to this project accurately. Forks that change behaviour materially should use a different name.

## Reporting misuse

Report abuse through [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) or the issue tracker.
